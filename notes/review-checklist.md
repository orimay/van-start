# Review checklist

Every learner complaint so far, turned into a check. Walk it per card and per screen.

## Card content

- [ ] Every Dutch word in a note, breakdown or example list carries its translation: `sterk (сильный) → sterkte (сила)`, both sides of an arrow.
- [ ] A derived word names every part it is built from, the whole chain (afgelopen ← gelopen ← lopen), and each part comes earlier in the deck.
- [ ] When the meaning does not follow plainly from the parts (af- + zeggen = отменять, af- + spreken = договариваться), a literal bridge links them: букв. «от-сказать», как «отказать». Each language writes it one way: ru `букв. «…»`, en `literally "…"`, es/pt `literalmente "…"`, tr `kelimesi kelimesine "…"`, ro `literal „…”`.
- [ ] A loan root sounds the same in every card that holds it (apotheek, muziek, hotel, video); an unstressed open o is `оо`, an unstressed open i a plain `и`, a plural -ieën `ии́йэ(н)` and drieën- `дриийэ(н)` keep the optional n; no doubled consonant letters, even across a seam (afval `а́фал`).
- [ ] The origin is the true one (gedachte = ge- + denken, not -te; leiding ← leiden; sleutel ← sluiten). An example given for an affix really uses that affix.
- [ ] An affix named in another card carries the affix card's own gloss (or a piece of it), and the gloss is plain enough to tell it from a near affix (-end делающий vs -ig какой). No two affixes share a gloss: affixes making the same thing (-es/-in, -te/-heid) name what picks one after `: ` and point at each other under `не путать с:`, in all 6 languages. Every sense of one gloss being a sense of another counts as shared (-tie `-ция: действие` was inside -ing's).
- [ ] An affix gloss names the affix's real meaning (-lijks = еже-), with one example per sense, and the example's meaning shows the affix's sense (not opleiding for "руководство"). A gloss naming a Russian suffix (`-ция`) has an example word showing it, and the note names the other suffixes it takes (`-ение`). What it makes is named by its part of speech (`существительное`), never as `предмет`.
- [ ] Note items read by kind: the rule about the card itself first, then where it comes from, then examples, each looking different.
- [ ] Every meaning of the word is given (haakje, lid, stranden, bladeren, agent).
- [ ] Close pairs say how they differ, clearly enough to pick the right one from the native side alone (aanmelden / inschrijven, voornaam / naam, thuis / naar huis, 's morgens / 's ochtends, lief / lieve).
- [ ] No two cards share a prompt within a language.
- [ ] A short form names its long form (het eind / het einde), a colloquial word names the neutral one (checken).
- [ ] The translation is accurate and literal first: tense and aspect (открывать / открываться, ходить / сходить), part of speech (утро понедельника, not в понедельник утром), register (тёплый vs горячий).
- [ ] Articles: nouns are learned with their article; the native side tells a specific thing from any (de krant lezen).
- [ ] Number: plural and singular are obvious (мн. ч.), the plural comes after the singular, -je after the base word. Irregular plurals (snelheden, apen, ogen) are marked too, and `мн. ч. от:` is the note's first item.
- [ ] A singular lists every common sense (blad: журнал too); its plural covers each one, or its note says `без мн. ч.` or names the other plural (bladeren / bladen).
- [ ] No hint in Dutch on the native side while the Dutch side is closed (jij kunt, aanmelden as a pair): Dutch in a note shows only once the card is open. That the card is a process or action stays visible.
- [ ] A closed card's example stays hidden behind Show context (a tap anywhere on the card shows it), so the word is recalled on its own.
- [ ] Examples use only words learned earlier (or about to come); an example sentence contains the word itself.
- [ ] Grammar terms are said in plain words with an example (be- = action aimed at something: лечить кого-то).
- [ ] No tense or verb-type names: `о будущем`, `op отрывается и уходит в конец`; the language is нидерландский.
- [ ] A confusable pair says `не путать с:` both ways, in every language.
- [ ] No gloss inside a gloss; a comma, never `; `, inside brackets.
- [ ] An example's translation matches the card's meaning (afspreken met = назначать встречу, not договориться) and the real-world fact (groep 3 = первый класс).
- [ ] No bare label stands in for a meaning: a particle says what it does (hoor: смягчает или успокаивает), an adverb label is used only where the word answers it (no `как «как? где?»` before `только`).
- [ ] A Dutch example in a note reads `nl — перевод` and sits in its own item, apart from the meaning (`просить; ik vraag om iets — я прошу о чём-то`).
- [ ] An affix's example is the first card built with it (a diminutive's `уменьш. от:` counts for -je).
- [ ] Rules carry their exceptions per card, the exception marked red.
- [ ] No `…` standing in for the headword; one item per line.
- [ ] Transcription matches the audio and the spelling (optional n marked, also after a diphthong: aardbeien, and a linking -ten-: erwtensoep; final a as аа: oma, pasta; één, maandelijks), in all 6 languages; audio is regenerated when text changes.
- [ ] Note items: a rule opens in plain words; an item opening with Dutch is an example, only its Dutch in bold (not a list like `dag (день), week (неделя): …`, not Russian).
- [ ] One spelling per sound across the deck: -pje/-kje/-fje as йэ, -tje as ьэ; the same word transcribed the same in a phrase as on its own card (maat 38 = achtendertig).
- [ ] A plural example agrees in number (de frites zijn); the example uses the card's own gloss (блузка, not блуза).
- [ ] An -en- after a vowel spelled with two Russian letters (eeuw → эйу) still gets its (н).
- [ ] -ig/-ige reads `эх`/`эхэ`; a word inside a compound sounds as on its own card (auto `аутоо`, garage `хаараа́жэ`, machine `маашиинэ`); h inside a compound stays `һ` (huis, heid, honderd); -air is `эр`, ou `ау`, an unstressed open a `аа`.
- [ ] All 6 languages carry the same change.

## UX

- [ ] Icons are outline SVG with stroke 1.5 like the rest, never emoji.
- [ ] Nothing jumps: hidden text keeps its place in the layout, an appearing button does not shift the screen.
- [ ] Buttons look like buttons, sit at the bottom within thumb reach, are sticky, and clear the screen's rounded corners.
- [ ] Each game page says what the game is and what to do; the game change is announced.
- [ ] Counters are right: "left" drops on a right answer, a miss adds the shows it needs, no jumps by dozens.
- [ ] A missed card comes back after 4 others, shows its progress ring, and can be marked right after a typo.
- [ ] Back walks every card that came before, to its last state.
- [ ] The keyboard stays up on typing pages.
- [ ] Text wraps balanced; no lone last word. Same font everywhere. Text readable in both themes (no white on white).
- [ ] Sheets have a visible title, a dark backdrop, block page scroll.
- [ ] Tappable word links look tappable and open the word's sheet.
- [ ] Continue shows the progress per game and starts at the first unfinished game.
- [ ] A heuristic on answers (slow, guessed, missed) skips the games that ask nothing: intro.
- [ ] Updates install quietly and land on the same step.
- [ ] A note never glosses its word's -e form as another word: the -e form is a pair card, a noun from it its own card.
- [ ] Every mention of another card is a labelled link (`от:`, `форма от:`, `мн. ч. от:`...); the target lists it back on its sheet.
- [ ] `scripts/audit-links.ts` finds no new hit: a word built from cards links its parts; a false split goes into `scripts/audit-links-reviewed.json`.
- [ ] Every -e pair card carries `форма от: <base> (<sense>)` in all 6 languages, and no `от:` of its own: the derivation sits on the base.
- [ ] An adjective whose -e form shows up anywhere in the deck has its -e pair card; its base carries the rule and `nb`/`nx` examples.
- [ ] An exception line (`!`) reads red on both sides, a Dutch line that is all head included.
- [ ] Every `nb` line has its own translated `nx` line in every language, `!` kept, never the Dutch itself.
- [ ] An affix meaning shown as the word (prompt, answer, option, tile) carries `(affix)` in the native language, never `(suffix)` / `(prefix)`, which would hint where the part goes.
- [ ] A card that scrolls shows its scroll thumb before, during and after a swipe.
- [ ] Things that belong together sit closer than things that do not: word and transcription closest, note groups apart, blocks further.
- [ ] Words sharing a root name it in their `от:` (onderwerp and voorwerp both from werpen).
- [ ] Feminine/masculine, compound/part, comparative/superlative/ordinal/base pairs link both ways in every language; a regular comparative or superlative also names `-er` / `-ste`; a look-alike sits under `не путать с:`.
- [ ] No affix gloss in a note says it adds nothing; it names what the affix changes.
- [ ] A derived word whose meaning does not follow from its parts (betekenen, ontdekken) says how it got there with `букв.`.
- [ ] A spec whose order comes from a date seed pins its day with fake timers.
- [ ] An affix with a hyphen never wraps away from its hyphen (`KeepAffixes`).
- [ ] A mix-up marks both cards missed, links them both ways, and brings both into the next decks together; the picker finds a card by Dutch and by meaning.
- [ ] A row in a sheet never runs past its right edge on a phone: a long chip goes on its own line under the word, and a meaning is never squeezed to a word per line. Check a sheet row with the longest real values at 390px.
- [ ] Typing another card's answer links that mix-up on its own when one card is written exactly so (`terug-`, `één`); a plain form with Ignore accents on also offers the marked one it lacks (`terug` -> `terug`, `terug-`; `een` -> `een`, `één`), a hint "x, y: all come back as missed" once taken for several; several matches open the picker's "The typed answer is" section, reopenable from the `≈` pill; I was right takes back only this step's links.
- [ ] Mix-up search lists the closest first: exact, then the query at a word's start, then further in.
- [ ] One reload after a deploy reaches the new version: a waiting worker gets `SKIP_WAITING` from the registration, never through `$pwa` (its plugin runs after ours).
- [ ] A mix-up keeps the card on screen; Remembered is off only for a mix-up picked on this step; a picked card can be unpicked; the other card comes back with cards between.
- [ ] Only after the answer shows (never on a typing retry), earlier links are listed under ≈, one per line, newest on top; the picker lists them first, unticked; picking one again misses both and keeps the link, taking it back keeps the link too.
- [ ] A miss counts the cards directly linked to it as missed and brings them back; a link of a link is left alone; a right answer leaves them alone.
- [ ] Every dialog keeps its actions and inputs in the footer, in thumb reach.
- [ ] Nothing hides under a notch or rounded corner: page edges, sticky bars and sheets keep `max(1rem, env(safe-area-inset-*))`; a bar at the page end replaces the frame's padding, not adds to it.
- [ ] Every text colour reaches 4.5:1 on its background in light and dark (red/orange: -700 light, -400 dark).
- [ ] Group settings open from the gear in the start menu title.
- [ ] Back closes open layers one per press, then on the first page asks before leaving; another back or Stay keeps the app and the next back asks again.
- [ ] White text on orange sits on orange-700 or darker (`contrast.spec.ts`).
- [ ] A link up a page is an `UpLink`, never a `NuxtLink` push: back on home asks to leave, it never walks through earlier pages.
- [ ] A sheet opened from a menu returns to that menu when it closes.
- [ ] Home is the last stop: after home -> settings -> a lesson -> home, Back on home asks to leave, never walks back through pages.
- [ ] A wrong typed answer strikes its wrong letters and puts the right ones after them (`_` until the answer shows), never a wrong letter standing in for a right one.
- [ ] Every mark in the typed-letter diff reads as what was typed: a space typed in error shows as `␣`, never a blank that a strike turns into a hyphen.
- [ ] Undoing a card's own answer (Later, Back) never drops the misses of the words it was mixed up with.
- [ ] An update reloads only at a move (card to card, page to page), never mid-card.
- [ ] A phrase with an article Russian does not show says it in words in every language (`(какой-нибудь)`, `(свой)`, `bir`, `(belirli)`).
- [ ] Every article in a phrase has a closing ru/tr note item, Dutch first, saying why that article (any, known, own, set phrase, amount…); en/es/pt/ro show it with their own article.
- [ ] A card's tint matches its kind: affix violet, noun sky, verb amber, else plain (`p` from `flag-kinds.py`), readable in light and dark.
